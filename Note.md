## 1. Project Overview & Architecture
This project implements a low-latency, real-time wireless Force Feedback (FFB) loop between a 1:10 scale RC Car and a professional PC Sim Racing Wheel (Thrustmaster base). 

### Hardware & Communication Stack
- **On-board Controller:** ESP32-C6 DevKitC-1 operating as a standalone WiFi Access Point (AP).
- **Sensors:** BNO086 V2 IMU (Orientation & Acceleration), ACS712-05A Current Sensor (Steering Motor Load), and an HX711 1kg Load Cell (Bench calibration).
- **Protocol:** High-speed UDP packet stream over WiFi (`S{steering} T{throttle}` and inbound telemetry payload).
- **Performance State:** Verified end-to-end telemetry loop achieves an average round-trip time (RTT) latency of **5.2 ms** with 0% packet loss at up to 40 meters, satisfying the <70ms haptic stability threshold.

## 2. Theoretical FFB Framework (Core Equation)
To maintain academic rigor and satisfy thesis review boards, the total force feedback torque ($\tau_{FFB}$) transmitted to the driver’s steering wheel is strictly partitioned into two categories based on the **Virtual Wheel Concept (Balachandran et al., 2014)**:

$$\tau_{FFB} = \tau_{Passive\_Resistive} + \tau_{Active\_Restorative}$$

### A. Passive Resistive Torques ($\tau_{Passive\_Resistive}$)
Forces that increase the physical weight or stiffness of the steering wheel depending on the driver's manipulation, but **possess no independent mechanical energy to move the wheel on their own**:
$$\tau_{Passive\_Resistive} = B \cdot \dot{\theta} + T_{fric} \cdot \text{sgn}(\dot{\theta}) + K_{stiff} \cdot \theta$$
* **Damping ($B \cdot \dot{\theta}$):** Viscous drag calculated from the wheel's real-time angular velocity ($\dot{\theta}$) to dampen oscillations.
* **Friction ($T_{fric}$):** Constant mechanical column resistance opposing the wheel's motion vector.
* **Centering Stiffness ($K_{stiff} \cdot \theta$):** A dynamic centering spring constant ($K_{stiff}$) that dynamically scales up as vehicle speed increases.

### B. Active Restorative Torques ($\tau_{Active\_Restorative}$)
Independent physical environmental forces generated by the vehicle dynamics that **actively back-drive, turn, or vibrate the steering wheel**, even if the driver lets go of the grip:
$$\tau_{Active\_Restorative} = \tau_{SAT} + \tau_{Gravity} + \tau_{Surface\_Jolt}$$
* **Self-Aligning Torque ($\tau_{SAT}$):** Restorative torque forcing the wheel back to center during high G-corners, calculated via front-tire slip dynamics. Includes a **Drift/Traction Loss Drop** effect.
* **Gravity Compensation ($\tau_{Gravity}$):** Active pull felt when the vehicle is traversing banking or uneven terrain slopes.
* **Surface Jolt ($\tau_{Surface\_Jolt}$):** Transients capturing high-frequency vertical anomalies (bumps/striking obstacles).

## 3. Physical Sensor-to-Formula Mapping
The software maps physical telemetry vectors sent from the ESP32-C6 into distinct components of the FFB math framework:

1. **Wheel Angle ($\theta$) & Velocity ($\dot{\theta}$):** Sampled locally from the **Thrustmaster Base Encoder** via PC backend (`pc_wheel.py`) to process **Damping** and **Centering Stiffness**.
2. **$\tau_{SAT}$ & Drift Estimation:** Derived via the **BNO086 Gyroscope (Yaw Rate)** and **Accelerometer (Lateral Acceleration)**. By evaluating the deviation between steering angle and real yaw rate, the algorithm identifies oversteer. When a drift occurs, $\tau_{SAT}$ drops sharply, giving an instantaneous tactile alert of rear-traction loss.
3. **$\tau_{Gravity}$ Calculation:** Measured via the **BNO086 Accelerometer (X/Y-axis Tilt)** through low-pass sensor fusion to isolate static gravity vectors when climbing slopes or banked curves.
4. **$\tau_{Surface\_Jolt}$ Isolation:** Sampled from the **BNO086 Z-axis (Vertical) Accelerometer**. Passed through an on-board **Digital High-Pass Filter** to strip away slow suspension rolls/pitches, isolating only high-frequency road vibrations to translate directly into the FFB motor.
5. **System Calibration:** The **ACS712 Current Sensor** and **HX711 Load Cell** are utilized during bench validation to map electric motor current directly against mechanical torque output, ensuring a linear torque profile.

## 4. Standardized Testing & Validation Methodology
To pass academic review panels, the "driving feeling" must be verified through a dual quantitative-qualitative testing plan:

### Part 1: Quantitative Objective Metrics
- **Latency Consistency:** Profiling packet delay over distance to guarantee a sub-10ms real-time haptic window.
- **Torque Linearity Curves:** Generating XY plots showing a linear response between target software torques and measured load-cell reactions.
- **Noise Suppression Performance:** Demonstrating that Kalman/High-Pass filtering algorithms decouple clean road surface vibration from baseline sensor noise without introducing phase delay.

### Part 2: Qualitative Subjective Matrix (Human-in-the-Loop)
Utilizing a specialized evaluation methodology derived from global automotive industry protocols (**JTEKT Automotive Engineering Standard No. 1021E**), test drivers must complete an identical obstacle track under two experimental states: **FFB Engaged** vs. **FFB Disabled (Passive Spring Only)**. Drivers grade sensations on a **1-5 Likert Scale Matrix**:
- *Center Feel Clarity* (Passive Resistive evaluation)
- *Damping Smoothness* (Passive Resistive evaluation)
- *Grip Limit Awareness / Drift Detection Response* (Active Restorative validation)
- *Surface Texture Realism* (Active Restorative validation)

**Ultimate Thesis Proof:** If statistical analysis shows significant improvements in track execution consistency, fewer collision errors, and lower tracking deviations when FFB is active, the thesis successfully validates its technical contribution.
